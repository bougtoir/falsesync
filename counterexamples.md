# Counterexample catalog (Phase 2)

Each entry: the naive claim, the counterexample construction, and what it
licenses the paper to say instead. All numeric evidence is generated by
`simulations/run_phase2_checks.py` (rows `CE_*` in `phase2_theory_checks.csv`).

## CE-SPIKE — Dispersion does not always soften the aggregate

**Naive claim:** the more dispersed the unit transition times, the smoother and
less break-like the aggregate.

**Construction.** \(F_\tau=(1-\varepsilon)\delta_c+\varepsilon U[0,T]\),
step \(g\). Then \(m'=A f_\tau\) has a jump of height \(A(1-\varepsilon)\) at
\(c\): arbitrarily large variance yet nearly step-sharp aggregate. Real-world
analog: a large cohort synchronized by a regulatory date plus a diffuse tail.

**What survives:** within regular unimodal scale families the sharpness claim
holds (P3). The diagnostic must therefore detect *distributional shape*
(spike/tail mixture), not just dispersion.

## CE-WINDOW — The LS breakpoint is window-dependent

**Naive claim:** the aggregate breakpoint is a stable property of the event.

**Construction.** Fix \(F_\tau\) (e.g., a right-skewed gamma timing law), fit
single-break LS on \(m(t)\) over \([0,T]\) for increasing \(T\). P7's FOC makes
\(\hat c\) depend on \(\bar m_R\), which moves with \(T\): measured drift in
\(\hat c\) of O(0.1·T-scale) in numeric runs. Same event, different window,
different 'break date' — publication bias landmine when windows are chosen by
data availability.

## CE-ALIGN — Realignment can worsen apparent asynchrony

**Naive claim:** aligning series to each unit's own estimated transition time
reduces spurious synchrony.

**Construction.** Unit-level \(\hat\tau_i=\tau_i+e_i\) with non-trivial
\(e_i\): event-time alignment recenters each unit at the *error-contaminated*
estimate, so the aligned panel displays jitter that exceeds the true jitter —
and if \(e_i\) correlates with weight or observation window (units with
cleaner fits get over-weighted), the aligned picture looks *less* synchronous
than truth. This motivates reporting alignment-diagnostics
(`event_aligned_summary` in the diagnostic spec) rather than naively
re-centering.

## CE-WEIGHT — Equal-weight intuition fails under weighting

**Naive claim:** aggregating more units smooths out idiosyncratic timing and
reveals the central event.

**Construction.** P4: weights exponentially tilted toward early \(\tau_i\)
move the aggregate LS breakpoint by O(\(\sigma_\tau\)) while unweighted
\(F_\tau\) is unchanged. A market-share-weighted or capacity-weighted series
can therefore 'announce' a transition before most units have moved.

## CE-UNITERR — Unit-level CP error mimics timing heterogeneity

**Naive claim:** spread in unit-level estimated breakpoints \(\hat\tau_i\)
measures timing heterogeneity.

**Construction.** Synchronous \(\tau_i\equiv c\) + noisy unit series: the
sampling spread of \(\hat\tau_i\) under unit-level LS fits is of the same
order as realistic timing dispersion. Without modeling the unit-level
estimation error (the S5 layer), estimated \(F_{\hat\tau}\) overstates
dispersion of \(F_\tau\). This is exactly why the diagnostic spec requires
`unit_breakpoint_uncertainty` and noise-model-based deconvolution rather than
raw \(\hat\tau_i\) spread (see `diagnostic_specification.md`).
